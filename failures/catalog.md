# Failure Modes Catalog

All failure modes across the 12 Porter skills. Grouped by recurring theme.

## Theme: Static Analysis

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F1 | Static analysis only | Listing current force strengths without forecasting trajectory | analyze-five-forces |
| F2 | Static capability analysis | Treating competitor capabilities as fixed instead of evolving | profile-competitor |
| F3 | Premature maturity diagnosis | Misreading a temporary growth slowdown as structural maturity | diagnose-industry-type |
| F4 | Neglecting divergence risk | Assuming historical competitor patterns will always hold | read-market-signals |

## Theme: Projection & Mirror-Imaging

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F5 | Projecting your own goals onto competitors | Assuming they want what you want | profile-competitor |
| F6 | Mirror-imaging signals | Interpreting competitor actions through your own logic, not theirs | read-market-signals |
| F7 | Confusing conciliation with weakness | Misreading a sub-optimal move as inability rather than invitation to cooperate | read-market-signals |
| F8 | Misreading cooperative moves | Treating a nonthreatening move as an attack and retaliating | design-competitive-move |

## Theme: Ignoring Structural Factors

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F9 | Ignoring government | Government works through all five forces; omitting it leaves analysis incomplete | analyze-five-forces |
| F10 | Ignoring the parent company | Portfolio role, capital allocation, and emotional attachments may matter more than unit management | profile-competitor |
| F11 | Ignoring parent company and government relationships | Easy to overlook but can be decisive | map-strategic-groups |
| F12 | Ignoring mobility barriers | Mapping groups without assessing what prevents movement misses the core insight | map-strategic-groups |
| F13 | Ignoring exit barriers | Even with scale advantages, competitors with lifestyle/emotional motivations won't exit | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| F14 | Ignoring competitor exit barriers | Failing to predict who is trapped leads to underestimating rivalry | strategize-declining-industry |
| F15 | Ignoring adjacent industry shifts | Evolutionary process #12 frequently overlooked but can reshape classification | diagnose-industry-type |

## Theme: Surface vs. Reality Confusion

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F16 | Confusing growth with attractiveness | Fast-growing industry can have terrible structure | analyze-five-forces |
| F17 | Confusing stated strategy with actual strategy | Always check if operating policies match public statements | profile-competitor |
| F18 | Confusing quality with technological leadership | Porter warns these do not necessarily go together | map-strategic-groups |
| F19 | Confusing cyclical decline with structural decline | Apply the structural vs. cyclical test before classifying | diagnose-industry-type |
| F20 | Confusing operational effectiveness with strategy | Efficiency improvements are not a generic strategy | select-generic-strategy |
| F21 | Over-reading a single signal | One data point is not a pattern | read-market-signals |

## Theme: Incomplete Analysis

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F22 | Treating all forces as equal | The governing force determines profitability; find it | analyze-five-forces |
| F23 | Listing without rating | Each sub-factor must be assessed for strength, not merely named | analyze-five-forces |
| F24 | Too narrow on substitutes | Substitutes can come from "businesses seemingly far removed" | analyze-five-forces |
| F25 | Skipping assumptions analysis | Blind-spot detection from assumptions is the highest-value output | profile-competitor |
| F26 | Treating all competitors as monolithic | Ignoring factions, parent mandates, and board dynamics | profile-competitor |
| F27 | Choosing correlated dimensions | Collapses strategic group map into a diagonal with no insight | map-strategic-groups |
| F28 | Making every firm its own group | Over-segmenting destroys the analytical power of grouping | map-strategic-groups |
| F29 | Using only one map | A single pair of axes may miss important strategic variation | map-strategic-groups |
| F30 | Insufficient data for classification | Don't classify with high confidence on 1-2 signals | diagnose-industry-type |
| F31 | Ignoring the medium | Treating a narrow-audience trial balloon the same as a formal public commitment | read-market-signals |

## Theme: Strategic Overreach

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F32 | Attempting cost leadership without scale | Leads to cost disadvantage, not advantage | select-generic-strategy |
| F33 | Differentiating on attributes buyers don't value | Pays cost of differentiation without the premium | select-generic-strategy |
| F34 | Focusing on a segment too similar to broader market | Broad competitors serve it equally well | select-generic-strategy |
| F35 | Hedging across strategies | Conflicting org arrangements guarantee mediocrity | select-generic-strategy |
| F36 | Seeking dominance when fragmentation is structural | Misdiagnosing a cause as overcomable when it is not | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| F37 | Defending monopoly share in emerging industry | Expending excessive resources to hold early share | strategize-emerging-industry |
| F38 | Pursuing Leadership without credibility | Competitors don't believe you'll outlast them; investment wasted | strategize-declining-industry |
| F39 | Brute force without finesse | Even superior resources fail against desperate or irrational competitors | design-competitive-move |

## Theme: Timing & Execution Errors

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F40 | Counting only visible entry costs | Ignoring brand franchise, channel access, proprietary tech | analyze-market-entry |
| F41 | Assuming pre-entry prices hold | Failing to adjust for retaliation-driven price depression | analyze-market-entry |
| F42 | Ignoring retaliation probability | Treating entry as if incumbents won't respond | analyze-market-entry |
| F43 | Overpaying for acquisitions | Assuming you can improve the target when your ability isn't distinctive | analyze-market-entry |
| F44 | Skipping sequenced entry analysis | Going for ultimate target when lower-risk stepping-stone exists | analyze-market-entry |
| F45 | Wrong timing bet in emerging industries | Pioneering when segmentation basis will change, or waiting when learning curve is decisive | strategize-emerging-industry |
| F46 | Single-scenario planning | Committing to one forecast in high-uncertainty environment | strategize-emerging-industry |
| F47 | Harvesting in volatile industry | Competitors immediately steal share when you cut investment | strategize-declining-industry |
| F48 | Divesting too late | Waiting until decline is obvious destroys liquidation value | strategize-declining-industry |
| F49 | Hanging on due to emotional attachment | Managerial pride prevents rational exit | strategize-declining-industry |

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F50 | Hollow commitment | Making threats without visible assets or irreversibility; bluff gets called | design-competitive-move |
| F51 | Ignoring mixed motives | Attacking head-on when flanking would paralyze competitor's response | design-competitive-move |
| F52 | Generalized retaliation | Retaliating broadly instead of targeting the aggressor; triggers chain reaction | design-competitive-move |
| F53 | No deny-the-base hypothesis | Defending without understanding attacker's performance targets | design-competitive-move |

## Theme: Industry-Type Specific

| ID | Failure Mode | Root Cause | Source Skill |
|----|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| F58 | Ignoring "stuck" opportunities | Assuming fragmentation always has deep economic roots | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| F59 | One-size-fits-all coping | Picking coping strategy without matching to cause of fragmentation | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| F60 | Internal inconsistency in fragmented strategy | Combining contradictory approaches (e.g., bare bones + heavy differentiation investment) | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| F61 | Relying on early barriers | Assuming proprietary tech will persist; failing to invest in scale/brand | strategize-emerging-industry |

| F63 | Shifting too late from advocacy | Continuing industry advocacy after penetration is established | strategize-emerging-industry |
| F64 | Misreading remaining demand | Assuming remaining pockets are price-insensitive when they're commodity-like | strategize-declining-industry |
| F65 | Ignoring fragmented overlay | An industry can be both mature AND fragmented; don't force a single label | diagnose-industry-type |
