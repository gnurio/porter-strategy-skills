# Heuristics Catalog

Porter-derived rules of thumb across all 12 skills. Organized by theme.

## Industry Structure (from analyze-five-forces, diagnose-industry-type)

| ID | Heuristic | Source Skill |
|----|-----------|-------------|
| H1 | Even a firm with a strong market position earns low returns if it faces a superior, lower-cost substitute. | analyze-five-forces |
| H2 | An industry with blocked entry and no substitutes can still have poor profitability if rivalry is intense. | analyze-five-forces |
| H3 | The extreme case (all forces strong) resembles perfect competition: free entry, no bargaining power, unbridled rivalry, commodity products. | analyze-five-forces |
| H4 | Exit barriers and entry barriers are often related; high exit barriers with low entry barriers = worst case (easy to enter, hard to leave). | analyze-five-forces |
| H5 | Buyer selection is itself a strategic variable — target buyer segments with less intrinsic bargaining power. | analyze-five-forces |
| H6 | Industry maturity does not occur at any fixed point — it can be delayed by innovations or other events that fuel continued growth. | diagnose-industry-type |
| H7 | Mature industries may regain rapid growth through strategic breakthroughs and go through more than one transition to maturity. | diagnose-industry-type |
| H8 | Fragmented structure can be a feature, not a bug — some industries have fundamental economic reasons for staying fragmented. | diagnose-industry-type |
| H9 | Declining industries can still be profitable if exit barriers are low and remaining demand pockets have favorable characteristics. | diagnose-industry-type |

|----|-----------|-------------|

| H12 | When management factions disagree on direction, sudden strategy shifts become more likely as power changes hands. | profile-competitor |
| H13 | Conventional wisdom that is no longer true is the richest source of blind spots. | profile-competitor |

| H15 | Every signal can be a bluff. Never treat any single signal as definitive without corroboration. | read-market-signals |
| H16 | The less damaging the chosen action relative to what was feasible, the more likely it is conciliatory. | read-market-signals |
| H17 | Fighting brands introduced with little support are warnings; those backed with full resources are offensive weapons. | read-market-signals |
| H18 | Private antitrust suits are the mildest signal of displeasure — treat as a cross-parry equivalent. | read-market-signals |
| H19 | Too much attention to signals can itself be a distraction. Focus on signals that would materially change strategic choices. | read-market-signals |

## Strategic Positioning (from map-strategic-groups, select-generic-strategy)

| ID | Heuristic | Source Skill |
|----|-----------|-------------|
| H20 | Profit potential is a function of (a) industry structure, (b) strategic group characteristics, and (c) firm position within its group. | map-strategic-groups |
| H21 | Structural strengths (mobility barriers, bargaining position) are relatively stable; implementational strengths (execution) are more ephemeral. | map-strategic-groups |
| H22 | Significant repositioning gains come when industry evolution lowers the cost of shifting between groups. | map-strategic-groups |
| H23 | Sequenced entry lowers total cost and risk by segmenting the investment: enter via reversible investments first. | map-strategic-groups |
| H24 | Cost leadership demands a firm be THE cost leader, not one of several. | select-generic-strategy |
| H25 | Differentiation does not mean ignoring costs — but cost is not the primary strategic target. | select-generic-strategy |
| H26 | Focus works by serving a narrow target "more effectively or efficiently" than broad competitors. | select-generic-strategy |
| H27 | Each strategy requires "total commitment." Half-measures produce stuck-in-the-middle outcomes. | select-generic-strategy |
| H28 | Different strategies require different styles of leadership and translate into very different corporate cultures. | select-generic-strategy |

## Industry-Type Strategy (from strategize-fragmented/emerging/declining)

| ID | Heuristic | Source Skill |
|----|-----------|-------------|
| H34 | Fragmentation is the DEFAULT. The burden of proof is on consolidation, not fragmentation. | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| H35 | It takes only ONE economic cause to block consolidation. All causes must be addressed. | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| H36 | "Stuck" industries are the highest-opportunity targets — consolidation is cheap because no structural innovation is needed. | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| H37 | Franchising is the canonical tool for splitting local-control requirements from national-scale economies. | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| H38 | Avoid seeking dominance in a fundamentally fragmented industry — it fights the industry economics. | strategize-fragmented-industry |
| H39 | "Only in rare cases will it be feasible and profitable to defend a near monopoly market share as the industry grows rapidly." | strategize-emerging-industry |
| H40 | Early mobility barriers (proprietary tech, unique product) will erode. The firm must find new ways to defend its position. | strategize-emerging-industry |
| H41 | The nature of entrants shifts over time — from small startups to established firms competing on scale and marketing. | strategize-emerging-industry |
| H42 | Conventional wisdom in emerging industries is often arbitrary, born from expedient early decisions, not analysis. | strategize-emerging-industry |
| H43 | Selling assets early recovers far more value than selling after decline is universally recognized. | strategize-declining-industry |
| H44 | Assets sold within the industry can worsen competition if the buyer operates on a lower cost basis. | strategize-declining-industry |
| H45 | The strengths that matter in decline are NOT necessarily the strengths that mattered during growth. | strategize-declining-industry |
| H46 | Harvest fails in volatile industries: competitors immediately seize on lack of investment. | strategize-declining-industry |
| H47 | Government subsidies keeping ailing firms alive are nearly as bad as high exit barriers. | strategize-declining-industry |

## Competitive Moves & Consistency (from design-competitive-move, audit-strategy-consistency)

| ID | Heuristic | Source Skill |
|----|-----------|-------------|
| H48 | Even with clear strengths, a war of attrition is costly to victor and vanquished alike — avoid it. | design-competitive-move |
| H49 | The more the competitor thinks you border on irrational, the more wary they will be. | design-competitive-move |
| H50 | Behavior communicated to suppliers, customers, channels, and the public all contribute to perceived seriousness. | design-competitive-move |
| H51 | Buyers may report secret price cuts that never occurred to encourage discounting — verify before retaliating. | design-competitive-move |
| H52 | If retaliation must be generalized rather than focused on the initiator, discipline becomes risky. | design-competitive-move |
| H53 | Assumptions surface inconsistency. Compare management's assumptions against industry and competitor analysis — mismatches reveal inconsistencies. | audit-strategy-consistency |
| H54 | Timing is a test, not a detail. Can the environment absorb the actions, and can the organization change fast enough? | audit-strategy-consistency |
| H55 | Societal concerns are not optional. Government policy and evolving mores can invalidate an otherwise consistent strategy. | audit-strategy-consistency |
| H56 | Values drive commitment. Even a well-designed strategy fails if key implementers' values conflict with required policies. | audit-strategy-consistency |
| H57 | Resolution requires data, not just logic. Fixing inconsistency means changing management's deeply held assumptions. | audit-strategy-consistency |
